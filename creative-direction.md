# Maxxed email banners — creative direction

14 banner illustrations across 4 narrative groups. Banners run at 1200 × 600 px (2:1 retina, displayed at 600 × 300). They sit between the centered Maxxed logo above and the body copy below, with no rounded bottom corners.

**Shared visual language across all banners:**
- Lime Neon `#67FA53` as the only accent color. Everything else is near-black, deep grey, or off-white.
- Instrument Sans Bold if any type lives inside the banner — match the email body.
- Sharp geometry over soft shapes. Picture the angled-chevron motif from the Maxxed logo: that diagonal energy can echo through every illustration.
- 3D objects rendered crisp and matte — premium product photography vibe, not toy-like or glossy plastic.
- No people, no faces, no hands. Maxxed reads more confident when the player is implied, not pictured.
- Don't put critical content in the bottom 40px in case of email-client clipping.

---

## Group A — Welcome (orient + brand)

### A-01 · Welcome to Maxxed
**The brand moment.** Their first impression of you as a brand. Reach for one strong object, not a busy scene. Ideas: a vault door cracked open with lime light spilling out, a single chrome key on a dark surface, or a stylized "M" gateway rendered in 3D. The angled-slash energy of the Maxxed logo should echo somewhere — maybe a diagonal beam of light, or the chevron repeated as a geometric pattern fading into the background. **Mood: confident, premium, the door has just opened.**

## Group B — Welcome Pack (pick → pressure → progress)

This is a six-email arc with a clear emotional progression: open invitation → friendly reminder → quiet last call → activation → almost-there → momentum. The visuals should track that arc, not all look the same.

### B-01 · Welcome Pack intro — three packs, one is yours
**Equal-weight options.** Three vertical objects side by side, no hierarchy: chip stack (Casino), match ball or scoreboard (Sports), diamond or safe (Highroller). Could be three 3D pedestals, three coloured ribbons descending, or three "slot" frames. All same height, same lime accent, no pre-selection cue. **Mood: invitation, fair choice.**

### B-02 · Welcome Pack nudge 2 — expires in {{days_remaining}} days
**Same three packs but time-aware.** Reuse the B-01 composition with a subtle countdown layer — a soft clock arc behind, sand falling between two glass cones, or a dim "DAYS LEFT" callout in the corner. Not alarming, just present. **Mood: friendly reminder.**

### B-03 · Welcome Pack final nudge — 4 days left
**Spotlight + countdown.** Drop the three-pack composition. One dominant element: either massive "4 DAYS" typography in Instrument Sans Bold, or a single pack object spotlit on a dark stage with the expiry date small below. Restraint here is the brand — the brief calls this "quiet-loud". **Mood: last call, no panic, decisive.**

### B-04 · Pack activated, no deposit — your {{pack}} is waiting
**One pack, one missing piece.** The chosen pack rendered alone — a treasure chest with the latch undone but lid still closed, a wallet open but empty, a 3D safe with the keypad lit but unentered. The "almost ready" gesture is the whole concept. **Three variants needed: Casino (chips + chest), Sports (ball + locker), Highroller (clean line art of stacked notes + a 7-day timer ring — quieter, no chest).**

### B-05 · Milestone 1 nudge — $X away from your first reward
**Progress visual at 70-80%.** A horizontal or vertical lime progress bar nearly full, with a flag, trophy, or reward chip at the unfilled end. Or a stylized track with a lime sphere rolling toward the finish line. Single focused image, no clutter. **Mood: push energy, you can see the end.**

### B-06 · Pack mid-progress — milestone {{X}} done
**Stepped momentum.** A staircase, ladder, or stacked tier of platforms with the first one or two lit in lime and the next one glowing/queued. Or stacked coins with the bottom layers converted to lime, the top still neutral. The visual should answer "where am I, where am I going" in one glance. **Mood: built momentum, ladder energy.**

## Group C — Loyalty (earn + celebrate + claim)

The loyalty group's emotional centre is "generosity" — Maxxed gives you something back. Don't oversell, don't show fireworks. The brand has 30 levels and 6 tiers — that scale is the visual story.

### C-01 · Loyalty intro — every bet earns something back
**XP wheel or orbiting rewards.** A circular six-segment ring (one per tier: Bronze → Silver → Gold → Platinum → Diamond → Maxxed) with reward icons (cashback symbol, wheel, gift box, calendar) floating around it. Or a vertical 30-level ladder with the user marked at Bronze 1 at the bottom and the climb visible above. Should feel like a system, not a moment. **Mood: explanatory, generous, this is yours forever.**

### C-02 · Level-up / tier-up — welcome to {{tier}} {{level}}
**Badge + ascent.** A 3D tier crest or medallion centred, with lime particles rising or radial rays. The brief calls for two visual sizes: **tier-ups get a larger, more elaborate badge** (think a heavier metal crest, lime gem inset); **level-ups within a tier get a simpler treatment** (clean medallion, single XP-bar overlay). Same component, two scales. **Mood: earned celebration, no childishness.**

### C-06 · Unclaimed wheel spin — you have a wheel spin
**Mid-spin energy.** A prize wheel rendered isometric or 3/4 view, mid-rotation with motion blur on the segments. Lime accents on the highlighted slot. Maybe an arrow pointer about to land. Could also be a coin/token mid-air about to drop into a slot. **Mood: kinetic, claim-me energy.**

## Group D — Leaderboards (race + win + hurry)

This group is the loudest in the system. Cash prizes only, fast cycle (24h daily, 7-day weekly). The visuals can lean into competition, speed, and money in a way the rest of the system shouldn't.

### D-00 · Weekly race announcement — new race, ${{pool}}, starts now
**Type-led prize-pool hero.** Make the dollar number the entire image. Massive Instrument Sans Bold "$50,000" (or whatever) with motion streaks/speed lines trailing behind, set on a dark surface with a lime underline or diagonal slash. Could also be a podium with a lime ribbon stretched across, or a stylized starting-line gate. **Mood: loud, fast, line-go-up.**

### D-01 · Daily race win — you finished {{position}}
**Personal trophy moment.** A 3D trophy or medal in lime + chrome, or the position number ("1st", "2nd", "3rd") treated as massive Instrument Sans Bold with lime confetti-like fragments suspended around it. Should feel like the player's win specifically, not a generic celebration. **Mood: claim-this-now victory, fast.**

### D-03 · Weekly race win — you placed {{position}} on the week
**D-01's bigger sibling.** Similar trophy/position concept but with more elaboration — a podium with the player's position lit up, a ribbon, a leaderboard board with their name in lime. The 7-day claim window means you have room to actually celebrate this one — make it feel premium and earned, not just transactional. **Mood: premium victory.**

---

## What's *not* getting a banner

The 13 transactional emails (E-01 to E-13) and 5 utility emails (B-07 Highroller inactivity, C-03/C-04/C-05 unclaimed cashback, D-02/D-04 prize expiry warnings) have no banner by design — the brief calls for "no images, plain layout, code displayed large" on the transactional stream, and the quiet-register cashback claims work better as text-only. Don't make banners for these; they intentionally feel different.

## Production notes

- **File names:** `A-01.png`, `B-01.png`, … `D-03.png`. Drop them straight into `/banners/` next to `index.html`. For B-04 variants use `B-04-casino.png`, `B-04-sports.png`, `B-04-highroller.png` and I'll wire variant logic into the renderer.
- **Format:** PNG with transparency at the edges (so it lives well on either light or dark email body), or solid-edge PNG/JPG if you're working full-bleed.
- **Weight:** keep each file under ~200KB to avoid Gmail's clipping at 102KB total email size.
- **Light-mode versions:** if the illustration relies on a dark background, also export a light-bg variant for the light theme (`A-01-light.png`). Otherwise the same file works for both themes.
